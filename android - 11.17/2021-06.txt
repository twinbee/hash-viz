Always use common sense and follow safety rules at all times.<br /><br />Be aware of swift water currents, water levels, weather conditions and water temperature. Prepare for changes in weather and the possibility of a capsize. In cold water, bring a change of clothes if you get wet. In warm weather, a long sleeve YakH3 shirt can provide sun protection.<br /><br />Beware of off-shore winds that make it difficult to return to shore (especially if you are on a SUP). Always watch for boaters in lakes and creeks nearby lakes. They sneak up on us fast!<br /><br />Brush up on self-rescue first in calm, warm, shallow water, and again in more extreme conditions. Yak hosts several safety events along with other local kayak groups. Please ask an admin if you have questions or concerns.<br /><br />Most importantly, WEAR YOUR PERSONAL FLOATATION DEVICE. Coast Guard regulations require that all kayaks have a lifejacket on board. Wearing your personal floatation device (PFD) will help keep your head above water and add insulation to your body, keeping you warmer in cold water. There are great PFDs designed specifically for paddlers. Buy one that fits well, and always wear it while you paddle. Bringing a whistle is also required. If paddling at night, bring bright lights to attach to your boat or a headlamp so others can see you (party lights are hash-acceptable).<br /><br />Stay hydrated. Always bring plenty of water and food...and beer!<br /><br />HARES: When paddling in a new area, check with the locals regarding currents and shoreline conditions. Plan an "escape" route - an alternative place to get off the water should environmental conditions dictate it.
