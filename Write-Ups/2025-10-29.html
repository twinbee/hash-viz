What is Harry without Hogwarts?

October 29, 2025
Hares: Lil Miss Blows Chunks

Likes It In the Kitchen
Wizard in Training

The Floo Network delivered an eager pack of Witches, Wizards, and Mischievous Muggles to Lakewood Landing for what was promised to be a spellbinding night of running and revelry, masterminded by Hares Lil Miss Blow Chunks and Pink Panty Poacher.

The call to don house robes was answered by a truly motley (and slightly inebriated) crew. Your humble male Kitchen scribe and his Gryffindor girlfriend, LGBT, led the charge, though sadly lacking in any kind of portable Lumos charm.

Our house affiliations were peculiar to the DUH:

• Gryffindor: (Courage, Nerve, Chivalry): LGBT • Hufflepuff: (Hard Work, Dedication, Loyalty): Likes It in the Kitchen, Pink Puke for Boobs • Ravenclaw: (Wit, Learning, Wisdom): Fruity Pebbles • Slytherin: (Ambition, Cunning, Leadership): Par For The Intercourse, Just Christian

And, making their debut, the glorious underdog:

• House Harriers: (Drunkenness, Getting Lost, Disagreement, Aversion to Cleanliness, Ignoring Direction, Love of Cheap Beer). Their colors are: Ugly and Orange. Their spirit guide is the Lost Idiot. This noble house was represented by Sleazy Rider, Joe (formerly of House Rugby, now committed to the true cause), Just Christine, Kiss My Arse, and My Little Tranny.

The 7:00 PM pack away was right on time, but the trail itself was straight out of a Defense Against the Dark Arts nightmare. Hares delivered a trail marked by a perplexing combination of invisible blue chalk and tiny flour dollops smaller than a quarter, that had tragically succumbed to the winds of change.

Your scribe, quite famously night blind as a bat, was saved from certain peril by a magical trio of kindness and headlamps: My Little Tranny, and the orange-clad Sleazy Rider, who provided essential illumination magic. This small cluster of runners clung together, navigating the repetitive Photo Checks that required a picture with every single arched window in all of Lakewood.

The Wand, The Wizarding Driveway, and the Repetitive Curse

Mid-trail, a true mystery unfolded: your scribe, distracted by a photo op, accidentally left his inherited wand—a family heirloom from his late Aunt, apparently a wizard in secret! Thankfully, the observant LGBT (Gryffindor, naturally) spotted the rogue magical instrument and successfully retrieved it, delivering it safely to the Beer Check. Ten points to Gryffindor for the save!

Speaking of checks, the planned Diagon Alley Beer Check was thwarted by a boorish Muggle proprietor. Undeterred, the pack executed a perfectly smooth and highly illegal BX in his driveway, secretly enjoying the butterbeer (and stronger concoctions) while plotting their next move.

Yet, this challenging trail held a marvelous surprise! Deep within the Muggle residential labyrinth, the pack stumbled upon a forgotten box of steins and dishes. A moment of pure, unexpected Hash magic! Every hasher, regardless of house or speed, was allowed to claim a souvenir trail treasure—a true bounty worthy of Gringotts! Many walked away with glass flagons claiming such far off heraldry as Amsterdam, Oak Cliff Brewing, Bavaria, and Italian handmade ceramic disheries.

On-In: Muggle Scrutiny and Sour Service

The pack eventually apparated to the On-In, ready for the revelry and the Costume Contest, which was, naturally, won by every worthy witch and wizard who dared to don their robes, including your Kitchen scribe and the lovely LGBT.

Throughout the entire affair, the Hares had cast a most peculiar enchantment: the Curse of the Singular Chant. The pack was only permitted to sing one song, recurring endlessly: "Something Wicked This Way Comes" (followed inevitably by the obligatory, cheeky interruption, "Come? Who said come? I'll take some of that!").

However, the On-In brought fresh challenges. The local Muggles seemed wholly unamused by the chanting, costumed crowd, and filmed the entire proceedings with their strange rectangular devices. Furthermore, the bartenders seemed afflicted by a severe case of Muggle-itis; all of our bar tabs were inexplicably scribbled with the name "Harry Potter," and the staff showed a distinct and deeply rude reluctance to serve any member of the wizarding collective.
